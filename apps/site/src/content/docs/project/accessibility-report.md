## Terms

- **Supports**: the functionality of the product has at least one method that meets the criterion
  without known defects or meets with equivalent facilitation.
- **Partially Supports**: some functionality of the product does not meet the criterion.
- **Does Not Support**: the majority of product functionality does not meet the criterion.
- **Not Applicable**: the criterion is not relevant to the product.

## WCAG 2.2 Report

The WCAG tables also stand for EN 301 549 clause 9 (Web) and clause 11 where the editor is part of
an application, and for Revised Section 508 501 (Web and Software), 504.2 (Authoring Tool) and
602.3 (Support Documentation). Level AAA is not required by either standard; it is reported for
transparency.

## Authoring support (informative)

Beyond its own interface, the editor helps people write accessible content (in the spirit of
ATAG 2.0 Part B; not a formal ATAG evaluation): the accessibility checker plugin finds missing or
file-name alternative text, skipped heading levels, empty headings, paragraphs that should be
headings, lists typed by hand, vague and duplicate links, low text contrast, duplicate ids and
tables without header cells, and fixes most of them in one step; the image dialog asks for
alternative text and allows marking an image decorative; the Language button marks passages in
another language; tables have header cells and captions; headings, lists and quotes produce
semantic HTML.

## Known limitations

- Screen readers: tested with NVDA and Chrome on Windows in every test run. JAWS, VoiceOver (macOS
  and iOS), TalkBack and Dragon NaturallySpeaking are not tested yet.
- Mobile: touch use is tested in mobile Chrome emulation, not yet on real phones and tablets.
- Windows high contrast is emulated in Chromium for the main states, not for every panel.
- No independent audit yet.
- Level AAA: see the Partially Supports remarks in Table 3.
